# Candidates — One-Star Review of Something Essential

Gate: one single-line deadpan one-star review, 1–160 characters, of an everyday
essential everyone relies on. No exclamation marks. Written like a real
one-star review ("One star. ...") in earnest complaint voice.

Judge criteria, in order:
1. A one-star review of an everyday essential, deadpan, unmistakably about the thing itself.
2. The sharpest true grievance — the small daily annoyance everyone recognises but has never seen written down.
3. Holds the deadpan: one star, no winking, no explaining the joke.
4. Turns in the last few words — the sting arrives after the complaint has been read straight.
5. Tight and specific, one joke told cleanly, no filler, no second joke.

## The field (14)

1. **Gravity** — "One star. Works perfectly every single day, which is exactly the problem. Everything I drop breaks, and it has never once caught anything."
2. **Sleep** — "One star. Takes eight hours to install and still wakes me up tired. The free trial has lasted forty years and I cannot cancel it."
3. **The sun** — "One star. Blinds me every morning, burns me every summer, and after all these years it still will not let me look at it directly."
4. **Water** — "One star. Tastes of nothing, ruins everything it touches eventually, and leaks out of every container I own except the one in my phone."
5. **Oxygen** — "One star. Been breathing it my whole life and I am still winded on the stairs. Also it is quietly rusting me from the inside."
6. **Mondays** — "One star. Arrives every week without being asked, ruins the two days before it just by existing, and has never apologised once."
7. **Memory** — "One star. It remembers every embarrassing thing I said in 2009 in perfect detail, yet cannot keep a single new password for ten minutes."
8. **Time** — "One star. Moves fastest when I am happy and slowest at the dentist. Customer service has not responded in several thousand years."
9. **Gravity (alt)** — "One star. Has held me down my entire life and calls it a service. I have never once been asked."
10. **Sleep (alt)** — "One star. I lie down, close my eyes, and it simply does not show up. When it finally arrives, the alarm takes it away."
11. **The sun (alt)** — "One star. Shows up late all winter, leaves early, and in summer it overstays directly above my car."
12. **Water (alt)** — "One star. I need it to live, apparently, and yet it comes out of my own eyes whenever I am sad. Poor design."
13. **Memory (alt)** — "One star. It stores the lyrics to a song I hate from 1997 word for word, but deletes the reason I walked into this room."
14. **Rain** — "One star. Falls on the one day I forget an umbrella, every time, and somehow never on the garden when the garden needs it."

## Duels (criteria applied in order)

- **2 vs 10 (Sleep)**: 10 is the plainer complaint and reads straighter, but its
  sting ("the alarm takes it away") is a situation, not a grievance about sleep
  itself. 2's "takes eight hours to install and still wakes me up tired" is the
  sharper true grievance; its "free trial" tail is a second joke, though, which
  costs it on criterion 5. Neither survives the semifinal.
- **3 vs 11 (The sun)**: 11 is tighter, but "overstays directly above my car"
  is a mild gripe. 3's refusal to be looked at is the more essential, stranger
  truth. 3 advances, then loses on criterion 2 — sunburn and glare are written
  down everywhere already.
- **4 vs 12 (Water)**: 12's "comes out of my own eyes" is a real observation,
  but "Poor design" explains the joke and breaks criterion 3. 4 is deadpan
  throughout; "except the one in my phone" is odd rather than sharp. Both fall.
- **14 (Rain)**: disqualified on criterion 1 — rain is weather, not an
  essential everyone relies on, and the umbrella grievance is a cliché.
- **8 (Time)**: the dentist observation is good, but "customer service has not
  responded" is a stock review joke transplanted onto time; criterion 5 fails.
- **5 (Oxygen)**: "quietly rusting me from the inside" is a genuinely sharp,
  true grievance and the sting lands late. It reaches the semifinal; it loses
  only because rusting is a slow, abstract harm, while the finalists' harms are
  felt daily and concretely.
- **6 (Mondays)**: "ruins the two days before it just by existing" is the best
  single clause in the field, but Mondays are a calendar convention, and the
  review spends its length on arrival and apology rather than one grievance.
  Semifinalist.
- **9 vs 1 (Gravity)**: 9 is the tightest line here, but "held me down" is a
  metaphor the reader has to convert. 1 complains about gravity's actual daily
  work — dropped things breaking — and saves "it has never once caught
  anything" for the last five words: a perfect criterion-4 turn, read straight
  until it stings. 1 advances.
- **13 vs 7 (Memory)**: both hit the same grievance — memory keeps the useless
  and drops the needed. 13's deleted errand ("the reason I walked into this
  room") is funny but familiar, almost a greeting-card line. 7 is more
  specific: a dated embarrassment held "in perfect detail" against "a single
  new password" kept "for ten minutes" — two measured quantities, one standard
  applied unevenly, and the sting ("for ten minutes") is the very end. 7 wins
  the pairing.

## Final: 7 (Memory) vs 1 (Gravity)

Criterion 2 decides it. Gravity's grievance — things fall and break — is the
oldest complaint there is; memory's grievance is the one everyone over
twenty-five feels weekly and almost never sees written as a product failure:
total recall for humiliation, ten minutes of retention for the one string of
characters modern life actually runs on. Criterion 4: both turn late, but 7's
turn is a measurement, and measurements are the native language of a one-star
review. Criterion 5: 7 is one joke, one standard, no filler.

## WINNER

**Memory** — "One star. It remembers every embarrassing thing I said in 2009 in perfect detail, yet cannot keep a single new password for ten minutes."

## RUNNER-UP

**Gravity** — "One star. Works perfectly every single day, which is exactly the problem. Everything I drop breaks, and it has never once caught anything."
